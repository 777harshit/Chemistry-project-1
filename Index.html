📘 Discovery of sub-atomic particles

Discovery of electron (Thomson’s cathode ray experiment)

In this experiment a gas discharge tube made of hard glass and fitted with metal electrodes was evacuated by a vacuum pump, and a high voltage was applied across the electrodes. Thomson observed that some invisible ray, originating from the cathode, moves towards the anode in the gas discharge tube. Since the radiation originates from the cathode, he termed it the cathode ray.

Properties of cathode rays

  1. They travel in a straight line.
  2. They travel perpendicular to the surface of the cathode.
  3. They are independent of the type of metal used as the electrode.
  4. They get deflected in a magnetic field.
  5. They contain negative charge.
  6. They are made up of small invisible particles.
Conclusion: Cathode ray is made up of a stream of invisible, negatively charged particles, which are named electrons.

Charge to mass ratio of electron

Thomson was able to determine the value of e/m as:

e / mₑ = 1.758820 × 10¹¹ C kg⁻¹

where mₑ = mass of the electron in kg and e = magnitude of the charge on the electron in coulomb (C). Since electrons are negatively charged, the charge on an electron is −e.

Charge on the electron

R. A. Millikan devised a method known as the oil drop experiment to determine the charge on the electron. He found it to be:
